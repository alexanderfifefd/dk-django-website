The collective runs on trust and habit as much as on software. That works when the same people have
been around for a while. It gets fragile when someone new arrives, when the board turns over, or when
we need to explain ourselves to a partner or funder.

## What we're aiming for

Governance documents don't need to be a legal treatise on day one. They should answer plain questions:

- How does someone become a member?
- Who decides what, and where is that decision recorded?
- What happens when responsibilities aren't being carried?

The governance documents initiative exists to keep that work visible until the answers are written down and agreed.

## Why an initiative, not just a Loomio thread

Some of this will be debated on Loomio — and should be. An initiative page gives the debate a stable
home on the public site: a summary of the goal, who is driving it, and progress updates as drafts land.
Articles like this one can explain *why* without mixing operational notices into the pitch.

## What happens next

Draft outlines for membership and decision-making, review with the board, then iteration in the open.
If you have opinions or appetite to help write, talk to the taker or join the discussion linked from
the initiative page.
